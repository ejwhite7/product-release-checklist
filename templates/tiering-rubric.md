# Launch Tiering Rubric

Not every release deserves a founder post, a webinar, and a paid flight. Running the full stack for every launch burns out the team, tires the audience, and leaves less attention for the launches that matter. Score each launch, then use the tier to decide which checklist items apply.

| Factor | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Reach**: share of active users who will notice or benefit | <5% | 5–25% | 25–60% | >60% |
| **Acquisition story**: would a prospect choose us because of this? | No | Marginal | Yes, for a niche segment | Yes, a headline differentiator |
| **Revenue link**: pricing, upgrade, or retention impact | None | Indirect | Upgrade trigger or retention driver | New paid SKU or pricing change |
| **Behavior change**: how much does the user have to learn or do? | None (it just works) | Small | New workflow | New mental model |
| **Competitive / narrative**: does it shift how the market sees us? | No | Parity | Leapfrogs a competitor | Category-defining |

**Total (0–15):**
- **T1 Flagship (11–15):** full checklist. At most one per ~6 weeks. Founder-led.
- **T2 Notable (6–10):** owned channels plus in-app, lifecycle, KB, and light social. No paid unless the launch has an acquisition story.
- **T3 Minor (0–5):** changelog, KB, in-app tooltip, support note. Bundle into a monthly "What's new" roundup (the beehiiv pattern) rather than announcing each one alone.

**Overrides:**
- If Behavior change is ≥2 but Reach is low, keep the tier but always add education (KB, tutorial, in-app guidance).
- Pricing or packaging changes are at least T2 for comms and support, regardless of score.
- A bundle of three or more T3s can be promoted to a T2 "release moment".
